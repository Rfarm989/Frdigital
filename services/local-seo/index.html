What Local SEO Covers

Six pieces of work. Each one moves you closer to showing up when it counts.

Your Google Business Profile

This is the listing Google puts on the map. I claim it, verify it, pick the right category, and fill in every field that gives Google something to match against.

Consistent Business Information

Your name, address, and phone number need to read the same everywhere they appear online. Small mismatches make Google less certain you're a real business, and that costs you.

Reviews That Keep Coming

Recent reviews and your replies to them carry more weight than a big pile of old ones. I set up a routine that fits how you already work, so asking stops being something you forget.

Pages for the Towns You Serve

A real page for each town you work in, written with something specific to say about that market. Not the same page copied five times with the name swapped out.

Local Listings and Links

Listings in the directories that still matter, and links earned from real local sources. Nothing bought, nothing that puts your listing at risk.

Tracking Across Your Whole Area

I check your ranking from points spread across your service area, not from one spot. You see where you show up, where you fall off, and whether that line is moving.

Common Questions

How long does local SEO take?

Most local businesses see movement in 60 to 90 days on the searches closest to them. Wider areas and more competitive terms take longer. Anyone promising you results in two weeks is selling something other than SEO.

Do I need a new website first?

Usually not. Most sites need fixes, not a rebuild. If your website is the thing holding you back, I'll tell you. Most of the time it isn't.

What if I don't have a storefront?

You can rank without showing a public address. Google still ties your position to where you physically are and how close the searcher is, so setting your service area honestly matters more than setting it wide. A bigger service area doesn't stretch how far you rank, which is one of the most common misunderstandings I run into.

How is this different from Google Ads?

Ads put you at the top for exactly as long as you keep paying. Local SEO builds a position you keep. Plenty of businesses run both, and ads are a reasonable way to cover the gap while the slower work takes hold.

What if I'm already showing up fine?

Then you may not need me, and I'll say so. Start with the common reasons businesses don't show up on Google Maps and check yourself against that list first.
