Why Isn't My Business Showing Up on Google Maps?

One of the most common questions I get from local business owners is why they aren't showing up on Google Maps. Here are the most common reasons, and how to sort out each.

1. Your Google Business Profile isn't verified

Before Google will show your business on the map, it needs to confirm you're real. That happens through your Google Business Profile. If you've never claimed and verified your profile, or your verification lapsed, your business can be invisible on Maps no matter how good your website is.

Start here: search your business name on Google. If no profile appears, or it says "Claim this business," that's your first fix. Claim it, complete the verification (Google often does this by video or postcard), and you're on the map. It's also worth making sure your profile hasn't been suspended. If Google flagged it for a guideline issue, it can quietly drop off the map, and you might not notice unless you check your profile dashboard.

2. Your business information isn't the same everywhere

Google trusts businesses it can verify. One of the ways it checks is by comparing your name, address, and phone number across the web: your website, Google, Facebook, Yelp, and directories. If your phone number is formatted one way on your site and another way on Yelp, or your address is abbreviated in one place and spelled out in another, those small mismatches make Google less sure your business is legitimate, and it may hold your listing back.

The fix is consistency. Pick one exact version of your name, address, and phone number, and make it identical everywhere. I've seen a listing recover within days just from cleaning up mismatched information across a few sites.

3. You chose the wrong business category

Your primary category is one of the most important choices on your entire profile. It tells Google which searches you should show up for. If a plumber is listed under "contractor" instead of "plumber," Google may never connect them to someone searching "plumber near me."

Pick the single category that best matches what your customers search for, not the broadest one you can find. A good move is to check the top-ranking businesses in your area and see which category they use. Then add secondary categories for your other real services, but keep it honest: piling on categories that don't fit only blurs what Google thinks you do.

4. Your profile is incomplete or inactive

A bare profile with just a name and phone number gives Google very little to work with. Profiles that are filled out completely, with your hours, services, photos, description, and service areas, show up more often because Google has more to match against. Treat your profile like a mini website: the more complete and accurate it is, the more Google trusts and shows it.

It also helps to keep it active. Add fresh photos now and then, post the occasional update, fill in your attributes (things like "free estimates" or "wheelchair accessible"), and answer the questions customers post. Google leans toward profiles that look cared for and current, not ones that were set up once and left alone.

5. The searcher is too far away

Google Maps is built around "near me," so Google leans toward businesses close to the person searching. That means the same search can show different results depending on where someone is standing. That works in your favor: you don't need to show up everywhere, just across the area you serve. Keep your profile complete and your information consistent, and that's right where you'll land, in front of the customers you want.

One practical tip: set your service area to reflect where you actually serve, usually around 10 to 15 miles for most local businesses. If customers come to you, think about how far they'll realistically drive; if you travel to them, how far you'll realistically go. One thing worth knowing: your service area tells Google the region you cover, but it doesn't stretch how far you rank. Your place on the map is tied to your physical location and how close the searcher is, so a bigger area won't win distant searches. Keep it honest and realistic, tighter in a busy area, a little wider if you're rural or offer something people will travel for.

6. Your website isn't backing you up

Your Google profile and your website work together. If your website doesn't clearly say what you do and where you do it, Google has a harder time trusting your relevance for local searches. Clear service pages, your location on the site, and content that matches what people search all reinforce your Maps listing.

A few things make a real difference here: list your business name, address, and phone number the same way they appear on Google, give each of your main services its own page, and name the towns you serve in your content. It also helps to make sure your site loads fast and works well on a phone, since that's where most local searches happen. When your website and your Google profile tell the same story, Google trusts both of them more.

7. You don't have enough recent reviews

Reviews are one of the strongest signals Google uses to decide who shows up, and it isn't only about the total count. Recent reviews, and how you respond to them, now carry more weight than a big pile of old ones. A steady trickle of fresh reviews tells Google your business is active and trusted.

The fix is simple, even if it takes some consistency: ask happy customers for a review while the experience is fresh, make it easy with a direct link, and reply to the ones you get, both the good and the not-so-good. That habit alone can lift you past competitors who stopped asking a year ago.

8. You have more than one listing

Sometimes a business ends up with two Google profiles: maybe one you created and one Google generated on its own, or an old one left over from a previous owner or location. Duplicate listings compete with each other and can leave Google unsure which one to show, so neither ranks well. You often won't know it's happening unless you look.

To check, search your business name and address on Google and Maps and see if more than one listing comes up. If you find duplicates, you can report them to Google to have them merged or removed, so all your reviews and signals point to one strong profile.

How to fix it, in order

  1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile.
  2. Check for duplicate listings and ask Google to merge them.
  3. Make your name, address, and phone number identical everywhere.
  4. Set the right primary category.
  5. Complete every field: hours, services, photos, and description.
  6. Set a realistic service area for where you actually serve.
  7. Ask happy customers for reviews, keep them coming, and reply to each one.
  8. Make sure your website clearly states what you do and where.

Most of the time, showing up on Google Maps isn't about spending money or gaming the system. It's about getting these fundamentals right and keeping them right.
