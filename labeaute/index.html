01The verdict

On 30 August, searched from your own address, you ranked first for "med spa" and second for "botox". That is the expensive half of local SEO and it is already done. The move to Skokie now puts it in contact with the cheap half, which is not done.

A 4.9 rating across 124 Google reviews is years of goodwill that cannot be bought or shortcut, and it is almost certainly carrying your map pack placement on its own. Twenty-seven of those arrived in the last twelve months, so this is a current asset rather than an old one. Underneath it sits an eight-page site that names its own city on exactly one page, publishes zero pages for any individual treatment, carries structured data on the homepage only, and, the finding that matters, sends every booking click into an empty system that renders a blank white page.

Your Aesthetic Record account is live and correctly configured, with your phone number, logo and a working payment key. It contains zero locations and zero services, confirmed against a control call on the same session that returned real membership data, so the emptiness is real rather than a failed login. Someone reads about PRX-T33, decides, taps Book Now, and gets a white screen. That is not a search-rankings problem. That is a booking you never hear about.

Behind it sits a second problem with the same shape: a complete second website for La Beauté, last updated around 2020 and hosted on a marketing company's server, is still live and indexed, and it is the likeliest reason four sources publish four different sets of your business hours.

The three priorities

1. Get the structured data and the homepage title onto the Skokie address. They still say Lake Forest, and they are the first thing Google reads.
2. Get online booking working, or take the buttons down. The booking page is empty and every Book Now leads to a white screen.
3. Update BBB, Yelp and Facebook, and deal with the old website at spa.jcidmsites.com. All four still publish Lake Forest.

02Critical findings

F0 · CRITICAL · VERIFIED 1 SEP · RECHECKED 9 SEP 2026

Online booking is empty, and the Google profile books through the same dead end

The site's booking link redirects to labeautemedspa.myaestheticrecord.com/online-booking, which loads and renders nothing. Querying its own API from the page: /clinics returns [] and /services returns [], both with a 200 status. The account itself is live and correct: /config reports "La Beau Te' Modern MedSpa", your real phone and website, a live Stripe key, and is_services_active: true. Aesthetic Record needs a location before it can display services; with zero locations there is nothing to render.

The Appointments link on your Google Business Profile goes to the same place. It resolves to /book/appointments?user_id=…, which redirects to that identical empty booking app: byte-for-byte the same page the website buttons reach. That matters more than the website version: someone who finds you in the map pack books without ever loading spalabeaute.com, so this path has no fallback. Verified 9 September 2026.

Caveat on intent: every Instagram caption on your own homepage says "DM us to book," and a ☎ Call button sits on every page. You may have moved to phone and DM deliberately. If so the configuration is not the bug; the website is, because it still fires eight booking buttons per page into an empty system with no message to the visitor.

F2 · CRITICAL · VERIFIED 2 SEP 2026

A second live website, abandoned in 2020, is still publishing your old hours

spa.jcidmsites.com is a complete second website for this business: same name, same address, same phone. The domain jcidmsites.com carries the meta author "Just Click It Digital Marketing", so this sits on a marketing company's server rather than your own domain. Its robots.txt permits crawling, it carries no canonical back to the real site, and Google has multiple pages of it indexed.

It is unambiguously the stale one: © 2020 in the footer, a COVID-19 Safety Plan section, "Make an Appointment" pointing at its own contact form rather than Aesthetic Record, and a menu missing PRX-T33 and the PDRN treatment the current site offers. Businesses add services; the site missing them is the old one.

And it is the likeliest source of the hours problem. The old site publishes Mon–Fri 10–6 and Sat 10–4. So does BBB, whose file opened December 2021. The current site's schema says Tue/Thu/Fri 10–6, Wed 10–3, closed Monday and Saturday. The old site is still live and still publishing the older set, and citations that scrape it will keep reproducing them. Someone searching "med spa open Saturday" may be getting their answer from a website this business no longer uses.

Unverified Why it is still up, and who controls it, is unknown. A subdomain on a marketing company's hosting suggests a prior vendor relationship, but nothing observed says whether that relationship ended, or who holds the login. Note also that the current site is actively maintained (its sitemap was updated 13 July 2026), so this is one stale asset, not a neglected web presence.

F2b · HIGH · VERIFIED 2 SEP 2026

The homepage has 297 words of visible copy, and looks like 6,600 to a crawler

Raw HTML body text is 6,663 words. 6,288 of them sit inside a single Duda Instagram gallery as captions and hashtags, hidden by the page's own CSS: .caption-text{display:none} on galleries carrying data-instagram without data-show-image-captions. Strip the gallery, header and footer and the primary commercial page has 297 words. Any word-count tool reading raw HTML reports a healthy page and misses this entirely.

F3 · HIGH · VERIFIED

Zero individual treatment pages, and a competitor with 5 reviews has 20

Every treatment lives as a paragraph on a shared category page. Nobody can rank a paragraph. Your reviews carry you in the map pack; they do not enter the organic results underneath it, and that is where a dedicated page for each treatment would compete. This matters more after a move, not less. A new area has to learn what you do.

F4 · HIGH · VERIFIED

Four pages stack 4–6 H1 tags each; two pages have none

On /our-services every treatment name is marked as an H1: six of them. Same pattern on injectables (5), laser (6) and body (4). Meanwhile /contact and /landing have no H1 at all. The page never declares what it is about, so the crawler has to guess, and the guess is spread across six competing claims.

What you had on 30 August

Searched from your own address, you ranked #1 for "med spa" and #2 for "botox", measured five days before the Skokie content went live. At your own front door, proximity is at its maximum, so those positions were being carried by relevance and prominence: your reviews, your rating, and Google's confidence in you as a business.

Prominence travels with you. Certainty about your address does not. That is the whole exposure here: the asset is intact, and the thing that converts it into a map position is currently contradicted by four sources.

The order to do it in

  1. The structured data and the title tag. Highest signal, smallest effort, and entirely within your own control.
  2. The pages carrying both addresses: contact, gallery, landing, privacy policy.
  3. The old website. It is a complete second site with the old address on every page. It needs to come down or redirect, and it sits on a marketing company's server rather than your own domain.
  4. The major citations (BBB, Yelp, Facebook), which then propagate to the smaller directories.
  5. Re-measure, once the above agree with each other and Google has had time to see it.

08What this review does not know

Limits, stated up front

  • Rank scanning was deliberately not run, and should not be yet. A business whose address disagrees across its profile, its website and its citations produces unstable results; you would be measuring the confusion rather than the business. The 30 August readings above predate the move and are the last clean baseline that will exist. Re-measure once the sources agree with each other and Google has had a few weeks to see it.
  • No current ranking data. No rank tracker or geo-grid scan was run. Every statement about competitive position here is inferred from page inventory and SERP composition, not measured.
  • No search volume or difficulty data. No Ahrefs or Semrush account was connected. The keyword table is a reasoned hypothesis.
  • Review counts are confirmed; what drives them is not. 4.9 across 124 reviews, 27 of them in the last twelve months, read from the Google Business Profile on 8 September 2026. Whether that flow comes from a deliberate review request or happens on its own is unknown, and it is the difference between a system you can tune and luck you cannot.
  • The duplicate-copy suspicion is unproven. The service copy reads like third-party health-publisher text. No plagiarism check was run, so it is not asserted as fact.
  • Speed figures are lab, not field. FCP 712 ms and 2.04 s load came from one desktop pass on a fast connection. Real Core Web Vitals for mobile North Shore users will be worse. Pull CrUX data before making a speed claim.
  • Nothing here explains why the second site is still up. The hosting domain belongs to a marketing company; that is observed. Whether a vendor relationship ended and who holds the credentials are unknown, and were not knowable from outside. That is a question for you to answer, not a finding for me to make.
  • The booking finding could be deliberate. Zero clinics and zero services is verified fact. Whether it was switched off on purpose is not; your own Instagram says "DM us to book" on every post, which cuts the other way. So it is recorded here as what was observed (the booking page is empty and the site still points at it), and not as a claim about anyone's intent.
  • Why no automated tool caught the booking problem. A correct link into an empty system still returns a 200 status, so every crawler, link checker and site-audit tool rates that path healthy. It is only visible to a person who clicks through to the end and then queries the platform directly. If a previous report told you the booking flow was fine, this is why.
  • The GBP was only partly audited. The Appointments field was opened and followed to its destination (see F0). Categories, Services, products, Q&A, photo cadence, post frequency and review keyword chips are all still unexamined, and for a med spa they likely matter more than half of what is above.

09What this list does not do

Everything above is diagnosis. Most of the individual fixes are small, and the order to do them in is written out in section 7. None of that is the reason work like this sits undone for two years. The reason is that you are running a med spa, and four of these need an owner rather than a reader.

The four that need someone to finish them

  • The booking catalogue. Restoring locations and services in Aesthetic Record means sitting with their support until the catalogue renders and then testing every button on every page. Nobody finds out it worked by reading about it.
  • The 2020 website. Getting spa.jcidmsites.com down or redirected starts with tracing who controls a domain last touched six years ago. That is one email or it is a month of chasing, and there is no way to know which from the outside.
  • Twelve sources, twelve claim processes. BBB, Yelp, Facebook, Fresha, Yahoo and the smaller directories each have their own verification. Correcting eleven of twelve leaves the contradiction in place, so this is finished or it is not started.
  • Sequence, and proving it worked. Order matters here: correcting citations before the old site comes down means they drift back to the old address, and re-measuring before Google has reprocessed the move measures the confusion instead of the business. Nothing on this list is verified as fixed until the map is scanned again several weeks later and compared against the 30 August baseline.

If you want to run this yourself, the sequence in section 7 is the one I would use and it is yours to keep. If you would rather it were someone else's job to finish it and prove it worked, that is the conversation, and it is a defined piece of work with an end date, not an open-ended arrangement.
